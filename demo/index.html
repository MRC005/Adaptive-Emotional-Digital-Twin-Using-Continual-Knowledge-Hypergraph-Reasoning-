Longitudinal measurement science · digital health

Does the yardstick move?

Nearly every study in digital mental health predicts a self-reported score and treats that score as ground truth. But a person's internal scale can drift. After ten weeks of a stressful term, “a little stressed” may not mean what it meant in week one.

If the scale moved and nobody noticed, an apparent improvement is measurement drift — and every effect estimate built on it is wrong by an unknown amount, in an unknown direction.

This system asks whether passive sensing — phone and wearable data, which never asks the person anything — can detect that drift. It estimates how much, reports what it cannot identify, and refuses to produce a number when the data cannot support one.

02 · Personal digital twin

A model of the instrument, not the mood

Each participant carries a persistent TwinState: their response-category usage, their fitted thresholds, their sensor→report slope in each epoch, their audit flags and their recalibration history. Persistence is load-bearing — the estimand is a ratio across epochs, so without a stored epoch 1 there is nothing to compare epoch 2 against.

03 · Context & hypergraph

Where the hypergraph earns its place — and where it does not

A context is several conditions holding at once: conversation low ∧ activity low ∧ recent trend falling. That is one hyperedge over feature-value vertices — conjunctive and exact, where a feature-vector distance is compensatory.

The honest result: the hypergraph estimator fails

The frozen method identifies ρ* through a continuous covariate. We built the hypergraph-native alternative anyway and ran the ablation. It is disqualified on two independent grounds: it does not hold its size when the truth is ρ = 1, and at a real 15% recalibration it reports the effect in the wrong direction. Its narrow interval does not rescue it — calibration is judged before precision.

05 · Results

What the estimator says on simulated data

On a cohort where the true recalibration is known to be ρ = 0.85, the estimator recovers ρ* = 0.914. That is attenuated toward 1 by construction — which is why 1 − ρ* is reported as a lower bound, never as a point estimate of ρ.

The method we tried and killed

Synthetic

The natural affine approach fabricates a −0.107 apparent scale compression on 5-point scales when nothing has changed. Identical on 7-point scales, so it is not a resolution problem — the level coefficient absorbs threshold saturation.

A regression test asserts this failure is still reproduced, so a future “fix” that quietly breaks the baseline fails loudly instead of flattering our method. An earlier round reported −0.19 from a harness bug; that withdrawn value is still reproducible beside the corrected one.

06 · Real data

Two datasets opened. Two datasets rejected.

This is the part most projects leave out. Both obtainable, publicly licensed longitudinal datasets were downloaded, schema-verified and put through the same strict audit. Neither could support the method, and no threshold was relaxed to change that.

The A3 failures are the interesting part. Two participants show Var(s) ratios of 13.06 and 13.60 — resting-heart-rate variance changing by more than an order of magnitude between epochs. Fitbit resting HR is an algorithmic daily estimate, not a raw measurement, so its variance can move for device reasons rather than physiological ones. Even the four densest participants fail, on A3 or a sign flip rather than on count — so more data would not rescue it.

And separately: the scale direction is undocumented

PMSys stress is a bare integer. The 1.4 GB archive contains no README, no codebook and no questionnaire definition. Nothing in the data states whether 5 means most or least stressed. Guessing wrong would invert the sign of every result, so the adapter carries the raw value through marked not a severity scale, and the pipeline refuses a primary result outright.

The finding, stated plainly: the method needs roughly 120 repeated ordinal self-reports per person, plus a documented scale, plus a sensor whose variance is stable across epochs. Current open longitudinal datasets rarely provide all three. That is a real constraint on the method, and now it has measured numbers behind it.

07 · Methods & reproducibility

What is claimed, and what is not

The model

Standardise the sensor within each epoch, then fit per person per epoch:

P(R ≤ k | x) = Φ(ck − βe·x)

ρ*p = βp2 / βp1

Pooled as a mean of logs, with a nonparametric bootstrap over participants — never over observations. The unknown sensor gain cancels in the ratio; that is the whole trick.

Honest limitations

  • No real-data empirical result exists.
  • The hypergraph estimator fails its ablation.
  • “Reasoning” is the weakest title term — one rule-based trust check, not a reasoning engine.
  • Cannot separate a changed scale from a changed behaviour–construct relation.
  • Underpowered at 48 participants for ρ = 0.85.
  • One of seven ablations; six of eight baselines.
